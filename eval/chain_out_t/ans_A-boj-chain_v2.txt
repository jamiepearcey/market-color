The Bank of Japan has kept forward guidance pointing to continued normalisation, saying it will keep raising the policy rate "in response to developments in economic activity and prices as well as financial conditions," and Uchida reiterated willingness to raise rates further if economic conditions align [1]. This stance reflects mounting inflation risks: a weaker yen and a spike in global energy prices pushed Japanese inflation above the BoJ's 2% target, with the prospect of rising salaries also fuelling price pressures [4]. Board member Sato added that firms are raising wages and prices more actively, so the inflationary impact of a weak yen may be larger than in the past [4]. Accordingly, the BoJ raised its policy rate to 1.00% — the highest in decades — as part of a gradual normalisation driven by inflation concerns [6][9].
