Bitcoin fell below $60,000 amid a confluence of macro and flow-driven pressures. A major factor was renewed US dollar strength, which hit a 40-year high against the Japanese yen (USD/JPY broke above its July 2024 high of 161.95), raising concerns about Japanese intervention and weighing on dollar-denominated assets like Bitcoin [3][8]; this dollar strength was tied to a hawkish Federal Reserve under Kevin Warsh, with markets pricing an ~80% chance of a December rate hike [2][8]. Persistent outflows from spot Bitcoin ETFs — roughly $4 billion in June, the worst month of the year — stripped away the institutional bid that had cushioned earlier drawdowns [1][2][4]. Additional headwinds included capital rotating into AI-linked semiconductor and memory-chip stocks and capitulation by 2025 top-buyers, leaving BTC down about 53% from its October 2025 high of $126,198 and at a 20-month low of $58,115 on June 26 [2][3][4].
