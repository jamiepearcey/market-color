The Bank of Japan has framed its readiness to tighten as part of an ongoing normalisation process, having already hiked the policy rate to 1.00% while pledging to keep "raising the policy interest rate and adjust the degree of monetary accommodation 'in response to developments in economic activity and prices as well as financial conditions'" [1]. Officials have reiterated a willingness to raise rates further if economic conditions align [1], reflecting a broader tightening stance amid mounting inflation risks [6] and a gradual normalisation away from ultra-loose settings [9]. The underlying driver is inflation: a weaker yen and a spike in global energy prices pushed Japanese inflation above the BoJ's 2% target, with the prospect of rising salaries also fuelling price pressures [4], and firms are now raising wages and prices more actively so the inflationary impact of a weak yen may be larger than in the past [4]. 

One caveat worth noting: the BoJ's own assessment is that price trends remain slightly below 2% [18], so the context suggests the tightening signal is forward-looking rather than a response to inflation currently being sustainably above target. The context does not give explicit BoJ statements quantifying how much further tightening is intended (markets were pricing only 22 bps by year-end [17]).
