The dollar's roughly 3% rise this year has been attributed partly to safe-haven demand during the U.S.-Israeli conflict with Iran [8]. A larger driver has been a hawkish repricing of Federal Reserve policy: a more hawkish-than-expected FOMC dot plot and a hawkish debut from Fed Chair Kevin Warsh upended expectations for rate cuts and boosted expectations for rate hikes, with markets now pricing tightening into year-end as inflation stays above target and the economy keeps growing [1][3][13][18]. The dollar has also been lifted by the AI-driven boom in U.S. equities attracting capital and a broad asset-allocation shift toward U.S. markets [3][5], as well as a widening U.S.–Japan interest rate differential that has pushed the yen to multi-decade lows [12]. These factors together carried the dollar to a fourth straight quarterly gain [13]. The context does not give a single decomposition of exactly how much each factor contributed to the specific 3% figure.
