Inflation has been driven above the roughly 2% target [2] by several forces. Energy shocks from the Middle East conflict pushed up costs, and because the transmission of energy prices through the economy is gradual, these concerns persist even as oil prices ease [3][5]. "Insatiable" demand for AI infrastructure from hyperscalers—who will "pay almost any price" for inputs and show little economic restraint—is also cited as an inflationary pressure [1][10]. The Fed's preferred gauge rose in May 2026 to its highest annual level since October 2023, and inflation has reportedly been "too high for the past five years," with core inflation described as stickier than headline [5][1][14]. The context attributes the elevation mainly to energy shocks and AI-driven demand but does not give a full quantitative breakdown of each driver's contribution.
