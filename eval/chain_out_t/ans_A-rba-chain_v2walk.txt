Markets pared back expectations for further RBA tightening mainly because the three cash rate hikes delivered in 2026 had already tightened domestic financial conditions, with the subsequent slowing in activity running broadly in line with the Bank's expectations [3]. The RBA minutes showed the Board viewing policy as "somewhat restrictive" and inclined to pause and assess, explicitly noting that "the hurdle for near-term policy tightening is now higher," with the tightening starting to transmit through channels such as a softening housing market and slowing housing credit [3][9]. As a result, investors expect the RBA to keep policy unchanged at its August meeting and now price only about 10 basis points of tightening by year-end [1].

One caveat: several context items point the other way — the RBA retained an explicit tightening bias and said rates could rise further to return inflation to target [8][2][10] — so the context frames the pared-back expectations as a shift toward patience rather than an outright end to the tightening cycle.
