Markets pared back expectations for further RBA tightening chiefly because three cash rate hikes delivered in 2026 had already tightened domestic financial conditions, with activity slowing broadly in line with the Bank's expectations [3]. The RBA minutes showed the Board now views policy as "somewhat restrictive" and is inclined to pause and assess, explicitly noting that the hurdle for near-term policy tightening is now higher [3][11]. Evidence that tightening was transmitting through the economy — a softening housing market and expected slowing in housing credit growth — reinforced this [3][6]. Cooling price data also helped, with consumer inflation easing to 4.0% YoY in May (from 4.2%) and consumer inflation expectations edging down to 5.5% (from 5.6%) [12][9]. Consistent with this, investors expected the RBA to hold at its August meeting and priced only about 10 basis points of tightening by year-end [1]. That said, the context notes the RBA retained an explicit tightening bias and had not ruled out another hike if inflation persists, so the paring back reflected a higher bar for further moves rather than a decisive end to the cycle [5][3].
