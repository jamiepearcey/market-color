USD/JPY is holding near multi-decade highs — around 162.30–162.65, the yen's weakest level since 1986 — primarily because of the wide interest rate differential between the U.S. and Japan, which continues to favor the dollar [1][5][6]. Even though the Bank of Japan lifted its policy rate to 1% (a 30-year high), the roughly 250 bps gap versus the Fed's 3.5%–3.75% range keeps the JPY carry trade in play as a tailwind for the pair [5][11]. Broad U.S. dollar strength reinforces this, with renewed U.S.–Iran hostilities fueling inflation fears and raising Fed rate-hike bets, and BNY noting investors are prioritizing portfolio protection over fundamentals [5][8][15]. Additional pressure on the yen comes from persistent capital outflows and escalating Sino-Japanese/geopolitical friction, which offset otherwise solid Japanese data [3][4][7]. Technically, the break into "uncharted territory" above 162.00 has drawn momentum buying, with the next upside target near the November 1986 high of 164.50 [2][3][14]. Notably, Japanese intervention has provided only fleeting relief — authorities spent a record 11.73 trillion yen (over $70 billion) in late April–May with little lasting effect [1][16].
