Bitcoin's drop below $60,000 was driven by a combination of macro and flow-related pressures. Steady outflows from spot Bitcoin ETFs — roughly $4 billion in June, the worst month of the year — stripped away the institutional bid that had cushioned earlier declines [2][4], with another $1.79 billion pulled in a single week [4]. A hawkish Fed under Kevin Warsh (with markets pricing an ~80% chance of a December rate hike) and a strengthening US dollar added downward pressure, as the dollar hit a 40-year high against the Japanese yen and raised fears of yen-supporting intervention spilling over into dollar-denominated assets like Bitcoin [2][3]. Capital also rotated out of crypto and into semiconductor and memory-chip stocks riding the AI boom [4], while analysts noted that Bitcoin buyers "lacked conviction" and that 2025 top-buyers were capitulating [3][5]. The result left BTC down more than 53–54% from its October 2025 all-time high of $126,198 [2][9]. The context explains these contributing drivers but does not isolate a single triggering event for the exact move below $60,000.
