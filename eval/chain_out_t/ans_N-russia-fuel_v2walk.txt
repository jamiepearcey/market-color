Russia faces persistent domestic fuel supply problems—queues at gas stations and shortages of certain gasoline grades—after Ukrainian drone strikes on refineries cut fuel production [1][2], with some refineries temporarily offline because they were hit by drone attacks [8]. Gasoline inventories have fallen to 1.7 million tons, down 4% year-on-year, and are being drawn down to stabilize supply, while refining capacity is already running at maximum [3]. To ease this shortfall, Moscow is considering keeping a zero import-duty rate on gasoline, which would encourage more fuel imports [3], and is relying on damaged refineries being restored so that fuel production exceeds June levels as early as July [3][8].
